KNOW WHAT YOU POUR

Our Oils: Purity & Technical Guide

A practical guide for home kitchens, chefs and bulk buyers.

These are general oil profiles, not certificates for our stock. Confirm the available grade, processing method and batch specification before ordering. Smoke points are approximate and vary with refining, quality and use.

01 / COCONUT

Character, or a neutral finish.

Grades & processing
Virgin oil comes from fresh coconut meat; cold-pressed describes extraction without applied heat. RBD means refined, bleached and deodorised, producing a milder taste.
Typical smoke point
Virgin: about 177°C. Refined: about 204–232°C. Use the supplier’s specification for your grade.
Fat profile
Rich in saturated fat, especially lauric acid. Use in moderation; it is not equivalent to purified MCT oil or a proven heart-health supplement.
Commercial uses
Virgin oil suits coconut-flavoured dishes and baking. Refined oil suits applications needing less coconut aroma.
Source: Harvard Nutrition Source ↗
02 / SUNFLOWER

Choose the fatty-acid grade.

Grades & processing
Cold-pressed oil retains more seed character; refined oil is milder. Conventional and high-oleic sunflower oils have different fatty-acid profiles.
Typical smoke point
Refined high-oleic: around 232°C (450°F). Do not apply this figure to cold-pressed oil; request its tested specification.
Fat profile
Conventional sunflower oil is rich in polyunsaturated linoleic acid; high-oleic oil is rich in monounsaturated oleic acid and offers greater frying stability. Sunflower oil supplies vitamin E.
Commercial uses
Dressings, mayonnaise and baking. Refined high-oleic grades are useful for foodservice frying where stability matters.
Source: National Sunflower Association ↗
03 / CANOLA

A versatile kitchen staple.

Grades & processing
Canola is low-erucic-acid rapeseed oil. Refined grades have a mild flavour; cold-pressed grades retain more seed character and require their own heat guidance.
Typical smoke point
Refined conventional canola: about 242°C. High-oleic canola: about 246°C. These are reference values, not batch guarantees.
Fat profile
Low in saturated fat and rich in monounsaturated oleic acid; also supplies plant omega-3 ALA. Replacing saturated fats with unsaturated oils can support healthier cholesterol levels.
Commercial uses
Dressings, sauces, baking, sautéing and frying with a suitable refined grade. High-oleic grades offer increased stability.
Source: Canola Council of Canada ↗

Ask us anything
Which oils do you supply?+

Our range includes pure coconut oil, palm oil, sunflower oil, Canola oil and RBD coconut oil. Ask us about the current availability and specification of the oil you need.

How are your oils sold?+

All five oils are sold by weight in kilograms (kg). Enter your required weight in the enquiry form, or leave it blank to discuss the quantity with us. We’ll confirm prices and packaging with your quote.

Can I enquire about a bulk order?+

Yes. Choose “My business” in the enquiry form, select your oils and add your approximate quantities in kg. We’ll discuss available options, minimum quantities and pricing with you directly.

Where can I see prices and pack sizes?+

Contact us for current prices, pack sizes and availability. Include your preferred oil and quantity in kg so we can prepare a relevant quote.

What colour is your RBD coconut oil?+

RBD coconut oil is typically white when solid and clear to pale yellow when liquid; appearance varies with temperature. Contact us to confirm the available specification, quantities and packaging before placing an order.

Can you deliver to my area?+

Add your town or area to your enquiry. Delivery availability, charges and timing need to be agreed with Balangoda Edible Oil Enterprises before an order is confirmed.

Does the enquiry form place an order?+

No. It prepares a message for you to review and send using WhatsApp or email. No payment is taken, and your order is only confirmed once you agree on the details directly with Balangoda Edible Oil Enterprises.
